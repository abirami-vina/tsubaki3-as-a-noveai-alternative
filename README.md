## Why Look for NovelAI Alternatives in 2026?

NovelAI is a well-known anime image generator that started as an AI storytelling platform and grew into one of the go-to tools for anime art. Its tag system gives precise control, its reference tools on earlier models can carry a character's look into new images, and its newest model, NovelAI Diffusion V5, handles natural-language prompts much better than earlier versions.

So exploring NovelAI alternatives usually isn't about fixing something broken. It's about finding a workflow that fits how you create. If you mostly make standalone illustrations, you may be perfectly happy where you are. But if you're developing a recurring OC, planning a comic, or building a VTuber persona, your needs typically start to shift.

Some creators want better control over detailed prompts and crowded scenes. Others want to reuse the same character without rebuilding her each time, or fix one detail without regenerating the whole image. Many want all of that in one place, so a single image can grow into a manga page or a longer story.

## What a Good NovelAI Alternative for Anime Art Should Offer

Every creator brings a slightly different checklist to a NovelAI alternative, but anime work tends to test a model in the same places. Getting one clean portrait is easy with most tools today. The harder part is getting the details right, keeping them right, and building something bigger from the result.

Here's a closer look at the criteria we used to judge Tsubaki.3 in each test:

- **Anime Image Quality:** Clean linework, expressive faces, and polished shading should come through without heavy prompt tuning.
- **Instruction Following:** A strong model keeps every part of a detailed prompt, including the small props and camera angles that usually get dropped.
- **Complex Composition:** Action shots, unusual angles, and characters interacting with their surroundings should still make sense as a whole scene.
- **Recurring Characters:** Your OC's face, hairstyle, outfit, and markings should carry into every new scene without rebuilding her each time.
- **Structured Layouts:** Manga pages and multi-panel sequences need several related images to stay consistent within one frame.
- **Editing After Generation:** Fixing a stray detail or changing an expression shouldn't cost you the parts of the image that already work.

It also helps when these steps connect easily, so one good image can move straight into the next stage of a project. With that in mind, let's see where Tsubaki.3 fits.

## Testing Tsubaki.3 on an OC and a Complex Anime Scene

Our first test was generating Nova herself. You've already seen the result, but let's take a closer look at the prompt behind it and what happened when we ran the same prompt through NovelAI.

```text
A young mech pilot with short platinum-blonde hair and a pink undercut shaved on the left side, cyan eyes, and a small black barcode tattoo under her right eye stands on a steel catwalk in a mech hangar. She wears a fitted white and orange pilot suit with a high collar and a black headset with a single antenna over her left ear. She holds her white helmet under one arm and grins confidently at the camera, the open cockpit of a large white mech behind her. Full-body shot from eye level. Bright overhead hangar lights with a cool blue fill. Anime illustration, crisp clean linework.
```

<img width="1573" height="1320" alt="Two anime mech pilots with platinum hair and a pink undercut standing on hangar catwalks, one from PixAI Tsubaki.3 and one from NovelAI." src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/016a1365-adcc-441e-a50f-995a8021b564" />

*The same Nova prompt produced a clean cel-shaded pilot in Tsubaki.3 (left) and a glossier, more heavily rendered version in NovelAI (right).*

Here's a closer look at how Tsubaki.3 handled each part of the prompt:

- **Hair and Undercut:** Her platinum hair came through with the pink undercut on her left side, exactly where the prompt placed it.
- **Suit and Headset:** The white and orange suit, high collar, and single-antenna headset all matched the description, and the model added white boots and gloves that suit the design.
- **Pose and Setting:** She stands full-body on a steel catwalk with her helmet under one arm, the open cockpit behind her and cool blue light overhead.
- **Barcode Tattoo:** The prompt asked for it under her right eye, but it landed under her left.

That tattoo miss was the most surprising result, because NovelAI placed it under the same eye. Both models seem to have read "right" from the viewer's side rather than hers, so if exact placement is key for your OC, describe the side as it appears on screen or lock it in with a reference image.

Also, NovelAI's version is more heavily rendered, with glossy shading and a detailed mech, though it cropped her feet out of what was meant to be a full-body shot. Tsubaki.3 went for cleaner cel shading and kept the whole figure in frame.

With Nova's design in place, the next step was a much harder scene. Using her hangar image as a reference, we described her sprinting across a rain-soaked launch deck at night, with crew members in the foreground, alarm lights flashing, and her reflection in a puddle, all from a low camera angle.

The puddle reflection was the detail we least expected Tsubaki.3 to get right, and it mirrors Nova upside down, helmet and all, right where her boot meets the water. She also stayed on model, with her pink undercut, barcode tattoo, and one-antenna headset all intact under rain and red light.

### Fixing the Camera Angle With a Follow-Up Edit

It wasn't flawless, though. The camera sits closer to eye level than the deck-level angle we asked for, so we used this result as a reference and asked Tsubaki.3 to lower the camera with a follow-up edit.

<img width="1152" height="864" alt="An anime mech pilot shouts as she runs through the rain, seen from a low angle, with two crew members holding glowing wands in the foreground." src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/32f3fd43-eb69-40ea-bf36-fccdb506ff8d" />

*A follow-up edit in Tsubaki.3 lowered the camera to look up at Nova as she runs, under a stormy sky with crew members on either side.*

The edit prompt was a single line.

```text
@image 1 from a much lower-angle camera, looking up at the pilot as she runs.
```

That was enough to change the whole feel of the shot. The camera now sits near the deck, the stormy sky fills the top of the frame, and her stride looks far more natural than in the first version.

The trade-off was everything the short prompt didn't mention. The puddle reflection disappeared, her barcode tattoo came back thicker, her headset picked up a boom microphone, and her determined look turned into a shout.

None of those changes ruin the image, but they show that Tsubaki.3 treats anything you don't name as open to change. Listing the details you want to keep, like her hair, tattoo, headset, and suit, is the simplest way to protect them.

For creators weighing a NovelAI alternative for busy compositions, these results are encouraging. Tsubaki.3 kept several layers of a scene in order at once, and a one-line edit fixed the camera. Just be specific about what should stay the same when you make that kind of change.

## How Well Tsubaki.3 Follows Detailed Anime Prompts

So far, Nova's details have carried across every image, but each of those scenes had her hangar image to lean on. To really stress test Tsubaki.3's prompt following, we went back to the beginning, with no reference image and one prompt packed with more instructions than anything we'd used so far.

We asked for a split-hand action, a specific expression, a crowded cockpit, a side-specific lighting setup, a camera angle, and two small details that are easy to lose.

```text
A young mech pilot in her cockpit. Platinum hair with a pink undercut, cyan eyes, barcode tattoo under one eye. White and orange pilot suit, headset with a single antenna and no microphone. Right hand on the control stick. Left hand raised to flip a red switch on the overhead panel. Gritted teeth. Holographic screens around her, one showing a red warning triangle. Orange cat keychain hanging from the dashboard. Rain on the canopy glass. Blue screen glow, red light on the right side of her face. Close-up, slightly low angle. Anime illustration.
```

<img width="1152" height="864" alt="An anime mech pilot with a barcode tattoo grits her teeth and reaches up to press a red switch, surrounded by holographic screens, with an orange cat keychain hanging nearby." src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/390d5d60-5cb3-411e-830f-d0a403a87cf7" />

*Tsubaki.3 turned a prompt with more than ten instructions into a cockpit close-up of Nova flipping an overhead switch as rain streaks down the glass.*

Tsubaki.3 followed most of the prompt. Her left hand reaches up to the red switch while her right hand stays on the control stick, and split actions like that are often the first thing image models blur together. The barcode tattoo, cyan eyes, gritted teeth, single-antenna headset, rain-streaked glass, and holographic screens with the red warning triangle all came through too.

A few instructions shifted to fit the pose. With her hand raised overhead, the camera dropped much lower than the slight angle we asked for, and the cat keychain moved up to the overhead panel instead of the dashboard. Her pink undercut also spread to both sides of her hair, and the rendering came out looser and more painterly than the clean linework in earlier images.

One thing that helped was writing each instruction as its own short statement rather than one long sentence. When an action, a prop, and a lighting detail each get their own line, they're less likely to blend together. If you're comparing a NovelAI alternative on how well it handles dense, detailed prompts, that habit makes a bigger difference than the length of the prompt itself.

## Can This NovelAI Alternative Handle Manga Pages?

Single images are only part of anime creation. Comics and manga need the same character to appear several times on one page, with each panel carrying part of the story. So for the next test, we asked Tsubaki.3 to turn Nova into a four-panel manga page, using her hangar image as the reference.

Here's the prompt we used:

```text
A black and white manga page with four panels showing the character from @image1 scrambling to launch her mech.

Panel 1: She jolts awake on a bench in the pilots' break room as red alarm lights flash, wide-eyed. A speech bubble reads "ALERT!"

Panel 2: She sprints down a hangar corridor, pulling her headset on as she runs.

Panel 3: She climbs into the open cockpit of her mech while a crew member gives her a thumbs up. A speech bubble from the crew member reads "GO!"

Panel 4: Close-up of her grinning face inside the cockpit. A text bubble from the suit reads "Nova, launching!"

Keep her face, short hair with the undercut, barcode tattoo, one-antenna headset, and pilot suit the same in every panel. Clean panel borders, screentone shading, dynamic manga linework.
```

<img width="768" height="1280" alt="A four-panel black and white manga page showing an anime mech pilot waking up, running, sitting in a cockpit, and smiling, with speech bubbles." src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/af055d03-2369-47cc-8580-c66fced22867" />

*Tsubaki.3 turned one prompt into a four-panel black and white manga page of Nova waking to an alarm, sprinting down a corridor, settling into her cockpit, and grinning at launch.*

Here's a closer look at how the page held together:

- **Layout and Flow:** The page came back with four clean, evenly spaced panels in a clear reading order, and each one moves the scramble forward, from waking up to sprinting and settling into the cockpit.
- **Speech Bubbles:** Every line of text is spelled correctly, including the longer "Nova, launching!", and each bubble sits in the right panel without covering her face.
- **Expressions:** Nova goes from wide-eyed panic to focus, then a determined frown, then a confident grin, which gives the page a sense of rising tension.
- **Character Details:** Her short hair, headset, and barcode tattoo appear across the page. The tattoo is clearest in the final close-up, and the undercut reads as a darker patch of hair in black and white.

The slips came from details the prompt didn't pin down closely. For instance, panel three adds extra crew members, including a small floating chibi face we never asked for, and the "GO!" bubble isn't clearly attached to any of them.

For creators looking at a NovelAI alternative for comics, Tsubaki.3 handled page structure, pacing, and short dialogue well. Background characters are the part to check panel by panel before you publish a page.

## Keeping the Same Anime Character Consistent Across Scenes

A character like Nova only becomes reusable when she survives changes in distance, mood, lighting, and camera angle. So we took Nova's hangar image back into Tsubaki.3 and placed her in three scenes chosen to push her design in different ways. Each prompt described the new scene and ended with a line naming the traits to keep.

For the first scene, we pulled the camera far back to see whether her small details would survive at a distance.

```text
The character from @image1 sitting on her kneeling mech's shoulder at sunset, legs dangling over the edge, eating a sandwich and laughing, her helmet resting beside her. Wide shot from below, the mech and an orange sky filling the frame. Keep her face, platinum hair, pink undercut, barcode tattoo, one-antenna headset, and white and orange pilot suit exactly as in the reference.
```

The second scene asked for a side profile that should hide her undercut and tattoo.

```text
The character from @image1 standing at a hangar window at night, watching rain fall on the launch deck, calm and thoughtful expression. Side profile showing the right side of her face. Keep her face, platinum hair, pink undercut, barcode tattoo, one-antenna headset, and white and orange pilot suit exactly as in the reference.
```

The third scene changed her mood and moved her headset out of its usual place.

```text
The character from @image1 slumped against a wall in the hangar after a battle, her pilot suit scuffed and dusty, headset hanging around her neck instead of on her head, tired but relieved smile, eyes half closed. Medium shot, soft overhead light. Keep her face, platinum hair, pink undercut, barcode tattoo, and white and orange pilot suit exactly as in the reference.
```

*Nova's reference image (left) next to three Tsubaki.3 scenes of her eating on her mech at sunset, watching rain at night, and resting after a battle.*

The rainy night scene produced the most surprising result. We asked for a profile of the right side of her face, where her undercut and tattoo should be hidden. Tsubaki.3 turned her to show her left side instead, keeping both details in view. It seems to have treated the keep line as more important than the camera direction.

A few smaller details drifted too. The headset picked up a boom microphone in the after-battle scene, and her proportions look fuller there than in the reference. Neither change makes her unrecognizable, but both are easy to miss if you're building a long series.

For creators exploring a NovelAI alternative for a recurring OC, these [character consistency](https://blog.pixai.art/en/pixai-character-consistency-3-beginner-methods/) results are promising. One clear reference image and a specific keep line were enough to carry Nova through very different scenes.

## Editing and Continuing a Tsubaki.3 Image After Generation

A good image is often just the first step. To see how far one Tsubaki.3 result could go, we took Nova's hangar image and edited it four times in a row. Each edit used the previous result as its reference, and all four stayed in Tsubaki.3.

<img width="2788" height="1000" alt="Five versions of the same anime mech pilot in a hangar, showing her original look, a worried expression, a blue and green suit, red night lighting, and a poster." src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/07e3d5bb-f234-4857-944a-d093f6569af3" />

*Nova's hangar image (left) went through four chained Tsubaki.3 edits, with a new expression, a blue and green uniform, red night lighting, and finally a recruitment poster.*

The first two edits were almost seamless. Her grin turned worried without anything else moving, and the new blue and green uniform even carried over to her boots, gloves, and headset trim.

The lighting edit is where drift began, since Tsubaki.3 redrew the mech and catwalk instead of just relighting them. The final poster rendered both lines of text cleanly, though the background changed again.

Small, targeted edits stay stable, while whole-frame changes tend to rebuild the background. For anyone considering a NovelAI alternative, the bigger insight is that one Tsubaki.3 image took Nova from a single illustration to a finished poster without leaving the model.

## Is Tsubaki.3 a Good NovelAI Alternative for Anime Art?

Tsubaki.3 is a great NovelAI alternative, especially if your projects revolve around one character. Across every test, it kept Nova recognizable, followed a prompt with more than ten instructions, built a four-panel manga page with correctly spelled dialogue, and carried a single image through four edits into a finished poster.

What sets it apart is that all of this happens in one browser-based model, with a reference image doing the work that would otherwise take long descriptions or extra tools.

However, Tsubaki.3 wasn't flawless. Camera directions sometimes needed a second pass, background characters and outfits could drift, and big whole-frame edits tended to redraw the scene around her. Naming the details you want to keep and making small, targeted changes solved most of those issues.

If privacy, storytelling, or tag-level control is your priority, NovelAI is still an option. But if you're developing a recurring OC, a comic, or any project you'll keep coming back to, Tsubaki.3 gives you a foundation that's easy to build on.
